How pressure is defined

Pressure = Balance × Clock × Wicket strain. Balance: runs needed against what an average side scores from here. Clock: how few balls are left in the chase. Wicket strain: wickets down beyond normal, first innings only. The top 20% of balls are pressure balls.

How the situations are defined
  • Phases: powerplay is overs 1 to 6, middle 7 to 15, death 16 to 20.
  • Tough chase: a ball in the second innings when the required run rate is 10 or more, with targets anchored to the official result.
  • Behind par: the batting side's score before the ball is 10 or more runs below the average score at the same legal ball, for the same innings, competition type and year.
  • High or extreme pressure (exploration tabs): the top 20% of balls on the version 1 pressure index, which combines match balance, balls left, wickets lost and match stakes. The Pressure ratings tab uses version 2: no stakes, and wicket strain in the first innings only.
  • Dismissals are credited to the batter who was out, so a non-striker run out counts against the non-striker. Bowler wickets exclude run outs and retirements.
  • Team run totals are estimates: the data pack does not store how many byes, leg-byes or wide runs were taken. Batter runs and wickets are exact.
